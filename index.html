Title

Desktop Robotic Arm

TypePersonal
ToolFusion 360
StatusPrinted and assembled

Overview

A servo-driven desktop arm I designed in Fusion 360, animated to check its range of motion, then 3D printed and assembled. Seven custom parts take it from the base to a single-claw gripper.

Design features

  • Rotating base plus servo joints at the shoulder, elbow and wrist, each servo held in a screwed-down pocket.
  • Truss-style upper link with triangular cutouts to cut weight while keeping it stiff.
  • Servo horns bolt straight into a 4-hole pattern on the next part, so each joint is driven without extra linkages.
  • One moving claw closes against a fixed jaw, so the gripper needs only one servo.

Motion study

Animated the joints in Fusion 360 to confirm the arm can rotate, reach and open the gripper without parts colliding.

Skills shown

  • Joint and linkage layout for a multi-axis mechanism
  • Integrating off-the-shelf servos into custom printed parts
  • Dimensioned part drawings
  • Assembly joints and motion animation

  1. 01Base

    The foundation of the arm. A pocket holds the base-rotation servo upright, fixed with two screws through the top tabs.

    78.0 × 50.0 × 68.0 mm

  2. 02Shoulder

    shoulder.stl

    Turntable bracket driven by the base servo through a 4-hole horn pattern. Its ring-shaped side plates cradle the shoulder servo.

    49.0 × 53.8 × 45.8 mm

  3. 03Shoulder Raise

    shoulderRaise.stl

    The long upper link. Triangular truss cutouts remove material while keeping it stiff. One end bolts to the shoulder servo horn; the other holds the next servo.

    161.0 × 45.0 × 45.5 mm

  4. 04Wrist Flex

    wristFlex.stl

    Forearm link that bends the wrist up and down. Horn discs connect to the joint behind it, and a diagonal web stiffens the open frame.

    99.6 × 45.0 × 45.4 mm

  5. 05Wrist Twist

    wristTwist.stl

    Short bracket that carries the servo that rotates the gripper. The curved cutout in the mounting plate clears the servo hub.

    65.5 × 45.0 × 45.4 mm

  6. 06Gripper Base

    gripperBase.stl

    Holds the gripper servo and forms the fixed jaw that the claw closes against.

    69.8 × 41.0 × 99.0 mm

  7. 07Gripper Claw

    gripperClaw.stl

    The moving jaw. Horn discs on both sides let it pivot on the gripper servo, and the tapered tip helps it grab small objects.

    104.5 × 20.0 × 46.9 mm

Mobile Surveillance Robot

TypePersonal
ToolFusion 360
DateJun 2026 – present

Goal

Fit a camera, two drive motors, a battery pack and electronics inside one sealed cylindrical body that rides between two wheels.

Design decisions

  • Split-shell housing that opens for assembly and service.
  • One motor at each end, driving its wheel directly with no gearbox.
  • Battery and controller centered on the axis to keep the center of mass low.

Title

Airport Lift System for Arthritis Patients

CourseEDSGN 100
Team4 students
DateSummer 2025

Problem

Travelers with arthritis struggle with long walks and carrying bags through airports. Our team designed an overhead monorail with individually powered, detachable chairs.

Process

  • User research with weighted needs; mobility ranked highest at 30%.
  • Concepts across seven themes, four screened with a weighted decision matrix.
  • Presented at Penn State's First-Year Engineering Design Conference.

My part: support beam study

0.061 inPeak deflection
4Materials compared
  • Ran Fusion 360 static simulations on candidate track cross-sections under hanging chair loads.
  • The dual-web I-beam deflected least, then steel beat aluminum, stone and plastic, so cast steel was selected.
  • Proposed modular track sections so airports buy standard pieces instead of custom builds.

Why it works

δmax = PL3 / (48·E·I)

Extra webs raise the area moment of inertia I, and steel has the highest elastic modulus E of the options, so deflection drops.
