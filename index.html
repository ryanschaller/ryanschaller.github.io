Title

Desktop Robotic Arm

TypePersonal
ToolFusion 360
StatusCAD + motion study

Overview

A servo-driven desktop arm modeled as a complete assembly, from the base to a two-jaw gripper, then animated to check its range of motion.

Design features

  • Rotating base plus servo joints at the shoulder, elbow and wrist, each servo held in a bolted bracket.
  • Truss-style upper link with triangular cutouts to cut weight while keeping it stiff.
  • Servo horns bolted directly to the next link, so each joint is driven without extra linkages.
  • Two-jaw gripper driven by its own servo at the wrist.

Motion study

Animated the joints in Fusion 360 to confirm the arm can rotate, reach and open the gripper without parts colliding.

Skills shown

  • Joint and linkage layout for a multi-axis mechanism
  • Integrating off-the-shelf servos into custom structural parts
  • Lightweighting a structural member
  • Assembly joints and motion animation

Title

Mobile Surveillance Robot

TypePersonal
ToolFusion 360
DateJun 2026 – present

Goal

Fit a camera, two drive motors, a battery pack and electronics inside one sealed cylindrical body that rides between two wheels.

Design decisions

  • Split-shell housing: two halves close around the internals, so everything is reachable for assembly and service.
  • One motor at each end, coupled directly to its wheel. No gearbox or belts to package.
  • Battery cells and controller centered on the axis to keep the center of mass low and between the wheels.
  • Camera port in the shell wall; large wheels keep the body off the ground.
  • Concept sketches settled chassis layout, wheel placement and camera mount before CAD.

Skills shown

  • Packaging components into a tight enclosed volume
  • Design for manufacturing: split housing, low part count
  • Multi-body assembly modeling

Title

Airport Lift System for Arthritis Patients

CourseEDSGN 100
Team4 students
DateSummer 2025

Problem

Travelers with arthritis struggle with long walks and carrying bags through airports. The team designed a solution around that user.

Process

  • User research: empathy map, persona, journey map, and weighted user needs. Mobility ranked highest at 30%.
  • Concepts generated across seven themes, four screened with a weighted decision matrix.
  • Selected an overhead monorail with individually powered, detachable chairs.
  • Beta prototyping split into energy, chair type, and structural beam studies.

My contribution

  • Developed the wrist-keyboard concept through two design reviews.
  • Owned the beam structural study: geometry and material selection by simulation.
  • Proposed modular track sections so airports buy standard pieces instead of custom builds.
  • Presented at Penn State's First-Year Engineering Design Conference.

Title

Support Beam Structural Analysis

ParentLift System
ToolFusion 360 static sim
OutputDisplacement, in

Objective

Choose the track cross-section and material that keep vertical deflection lowest under hanging chair loads.

0.061 inPeak deflection
4Materials compared
2Webs in final section

Method

  • Modeled candidate cross-sections and ran static simulations with downward loads representing a loaded chair.
  • Compared peak vertical displacement across geometries.
  • Re-ran the best geometry in steel, aluminum, stone and plastic.

Results

  • The dual-web modified I-beam had the least displacement of the geometries tested.
  • Steel had the least displacement of the materials tested, so cast steel was selected.

Why it works

For a simply supported beam with a center load:

δmax = PL3 / (48·E·I)

Deflection falls as E·I rises. Extra webs raise the area moment of inertia I, and steel has the highest elastic modulus E of the options, at the cost of more weight and material.

Next steps

  • Check the simulation against a hand calculation from beam theory.
  • Define the full load case (chair, two riders, baggage, safety factor) and check stress against yield strength.
